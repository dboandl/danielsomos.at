I find a gentle approach important. It is about coming into contact with inner parts in order to find one's center and draw new vitality from it.

In good contact with yourself, you feel your strength. You can shape your life, take up space, and be present. Creative methods such as painting, drawing, sculpting, and other forms of expression can be helpful gateways—however, they are always an invitation, not a requirement.

Everyone carries stressful experiences within them. These deserve to be honored. Together, we create a safe space to understand these parts, support nervous system regulation, and cultivate inner security.

A personal experience: The inner anchor

I would like to share an example from my own experience to illustrate how imagination can open up new possibilities for action. I call it the ‘deeply relaxed pig’—an inner image that helps me stay connected to myself in demanding moments.

In stressful situations, we often tend to withdraw internally or lose our sense of presence. My intention was to find a way to remain stable and grounded in the here and now. The image of a pig gently resting its head on my foot and falling asleep there became helpful to me.

The pleasant weight on my foot acts as an anchor. It evokes a sense of familiarity and connection within me—an experience that serves as a supportive counterbalance to the stress arising in the moment.

By allowing both to coexist—the strain and the calming anchor—my nervous system can begin to regulate. I no longer need to withdraw, but can remain present and in connection, both with myself and with the other person. Inner images like this can become powerful resources. Together, we can discover and develop such images in a supportive way, without pressure and at your own pace.
