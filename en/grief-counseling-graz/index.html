Grief Counseling in Graz & online

When a loss pulls the ground from under your feet, you don't have to go through it alone.

Perhaps someone you love has died. Perhaps a relationship has ended, a life dream has broken apart, or you have lost something that gave you stability. Grief is the natural response to loss – and it needs space, time, and safety.

I accompany you through your grieving process: mindfully, body-oriented, and at your own pace. With me, your grief is allowed to be exactly as it is – loud or quiet, chaotic or numb. There is no "grieving correctly" here, and no timeline.

It does not matter whether the loss is recent or happened years or even decades ago. Grief has no expiry date – and it is never too late to give it the space it may not have been given back then.

Losses I accompany you through

Grief does not only arise from death. Every painful loss deserves space here:

  • Death of a partner, a parent, or a loved one
  • Death of your own child – bereaved parents
  • Miscarriage and stillbirth
  • Grief after the suicide of a family member – support for those left behind
  • Accompanying relatives of dying people and saying goodbye
  • Separation, divorce, and the pain of a relationship ending
  • Job loss, retirement, and letting go of life plans
  • Loss of home country, health, or plans for the future

Signs your grief may need support

Many people come to me while still functioning in everyday life, yet feeling inside: it can't go on like this. Maybe you recognize some of this:

  • Inner emptiness and the feeling of not being able to feel anything anymore
  • Numbness – standing beside yourself, living behind a pane of glass
  • Circling thoughts and rumination that won't stop
  • Exhaustion – everything is too much
  • When grief robs you of sleep and rest won't come
  • Loneliness and the feeling that nobody understands
  • Guilt, anger, or fear of the future
  • The feeling that your grief is stuck and won't move on
  • An old loss that keeps resurfacing even after years – or was never allowed to be grieved
  • A crisis of meaning – asking "what is it all for?"

All of these are normal responses to loss – not a sign of weakness, and no reason for shame.

Growing around grief

An image that shapes my work comes from grief researcher Lois Tonkin: we don't have to "get over" grief or leave it behind. Grief doesn't shrink – but our life is allowed to grow around it again. What at first fills all the space gradually gains new life around it: moments of joy, connection, and vitality.

The pain is allowed to remain a part of you – as a sign of the love and the meaning this person or this life plan held for you. Over time it changes: it becomes quieter, softer, and may only surface in waves – on anniversaries, with a song, a scent.

And just as important: you are allowed to let go. Laughing again, making new plans, and feeling life is not a betrayal of what you have lost. In our sessions, both are allowed to exist side by side – the bond with what you lost, and your growing onward.

How I work in grief counseling

My support is body-oriented and nervous-system-oriented: grief is not just an emotion, it lives in the whole body. With elements from Somatic Experiencing (an ongoing advanced training that shapes my work), I support you in calming your nervous system, finding stability in your body, and allowing feelings in manageable steps – without overwhelm.

When words fail, creative approaches such as painting and shaping (from my 5-year training in art therapy) can give form to the unspeakable – always an invitation, never a requirement.

I also bring experience with dying, death, and grief from my work as a hospice companion (since 2018) and from accompanying families in mobile children's hospice care.

In our sessions, it is about:

  • Finding a safe place for all feelings – including the contradictory ones
  • Sensing your inner home again, despite the loss
  • Activating resources that give you support
  • Step by step, inviting vitality and joy back into life – without bypassing the loss

Frequently asked questions about grief counseling

What is grief counseling – and what is it not?

Grief counseling is a protected space where your grief is allowed to be exactly as it is – without a timeline and without judgment. It is not psychotherapy and not a medical treatment: if I notice signs of an illness, I will support you in finding appropriate help.

Is it normal that I feel nothing after my loss?

Yes. Inner emptiness, numbness, or feeling like you are standing beside yourself are common protective responses of the nervous system after a loss. In our sessions, we create safety so feelings can gently find their place again – at your own pace.

How long does grief last – am I doing something wrong?

Grief has no timeline. Everyone grieves differently, and there is no "right" or "wrong." If your grief feels stuck or daily life has not been working for a long time, we look together at what gives you support – and whether an additional psychotherapeutic assessment would be helpful.

My loss happened years ago – is it too late for grief counseling?

No, it is never too late. Grief has no expiry date. Many people come years or even decades after a loss – often because back then there was no time, no strength, or no space to grieve. An old loss is also allowed to receive the space it needs, now.

Do you also support people after separation, miscarriage, or job loss?

Yes. Grief does not only arise from the death of a loved one. Separation and divorce, miscarriage and stillbirth, job loss, retirement, or letting go of life plans are also losses that need space.

Do you offer grief counseling online and in English?

Yes. Sessions take place at my practice in Graz (Rankengasse 31) or online via video – in English or German.

Note: Counseling (Lebens- und Sozialberatung) does not replace psychotherapy or medical/psychological treatment. If I notice signs of an illness, I will support you in finding appropriate help.
